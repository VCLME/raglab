How it's measured

A RAG demo always looks good on the questions its author picked. raglab is measured on question sets frozen before any tuning, and a component ships only if it moves those numbers.

Five rules

  1. Frozen questions. 500 French questions from PIAF and 277 Portuguese questions on Brazilian federal law are committed to the repository, sampled with a fixed seed, and never regenerated between two runs.
  2. Full haystack. The whole corpus is indexed (761 Wikipedia paragraphs; 252 laws, 4.6 million characters), so the right passage has to be found among thousands of chunks, not among the handful attached to the sampled questions.
  3. Passage-level relevance. Each question carries the character span of its answer. A retrieved chunk counts only if it overlaps that span: the right law but the wrong article is a miss. Chunking strategies are judged on the same passages.
  4. Independent judge. Answers are generated by qwen2.5:7b and scored by Gemma 4 26B-A4B, a model from another family, so the generator never grades itself.
  5. One change per run. Each run records its exact command, git commit, models and hardware, and changes a single thing compared with the previous one.

Three kinds of legal questions

The Brazilian-law set mixes three question types in roughly equal parts, because they stress different parts of retrieval:

TypeThe question containsIt tests
paraphrasethe passage's ideas in other words, no article or law numbersemantic search
referencethe article and law number (“art. 49 of Law 8.078”)exact identifiers, which embeddings blur
entityan acronym or proper name from the passage (FGTS, INSS…)rare terms

Questions were written by Qwen3.6-35B-A3B from passages of the laws, kept only if the quoted answer appears word for word in the passage, then filtered by rules derived from a manual review of 40 questions (23 of 300 rejected).

Checking the judge

A score from an LLM judge is only worth something if the judge is reliable. raglab builds 150 answers whose correct verdict is known (faithful, fabricated, a correct answer with an invented detail added, on-topic, off-topic) from 30 PIAF paragraphs, and has each candidate judge score them. A verdict counts as positive only for a full score.

{{calibration_table}}

On faithfulness both judges are close; Gemma is used so the generator does not grade itself, and because qwen2.5 wrongly penalises most short correct answers on relevancy. Gemma is fooled 3 times in 10 by an answer of the right shape to a different question. Calibration is French-only and uses synthetic errors, so it is an optimistic bound.

Reproduce a run

raglab bench all --label my-run
raglab bench report baseline-v0 my-run

bench all checks the LexML corpus against its manifest, ingests and indexes each corpus, runs retrieval and answer scoring, then writes benchmarks/results/my-run.json with the figures, machine, versions, model hashes and exact command.
